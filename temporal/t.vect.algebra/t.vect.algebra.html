DESCRIPTION

t.vect.algebra performs temporal and spatial overlay and buffer functions on space time vector datasets (STVDS) by using the temporal vector algebra. New STVDS can be created, which are expressions of existing STVDS.

The module expects an expression as input parameter in the following form: 

"result = expression"
The statement structure is similar to r.mapcalc, see r.mapcalc. Where result represents the name of a space time dataset (STVDS) that will contain the result of the calculation that is given as expression on the right side of the equality sign. These expression can be any valid or nested combination of temporal operations and functions that are provided by the temporal vector algebra.
The algebra provides methods for map selection from STDS based on their temporal relations. It is also possible to temporally shift maps, to create temporal buffer and to snap time instances to create a valid temporal topology. Furthermore expressions can be nested and evaluated in conditional statements (if, else statements). Within if-statements the algebra provides temporal variables like start time, end time, day of year, time differences or number of maps per time interval to build up conditions. These operations can be assigned to space time datasets or to the results of operations between space time datasets.

As default, topological relationships between space time datasets will be evaluated only temporal. The -s flag additionally activates the evaluation of the spatial topology of the maps. It applies wherever the algebra evaluates the topology of maps: in selections, in the hash operator, in the conditions of if statements and in comparison operators. Two temporally related maps are then only used together if they are also spatially related, that is the spatial extents of the maps are equivalent, one covers or is covered by the other, they overlap, one lies in the other, one contains the other, or they meet (the spatial relations equivalent, cover, covered, overlap, in, contain and meet). These relations can also be named in the curly brackets of an operator, for example {&,equal|overlap}; the maps then need this spatial relation in addition to the temporal one. Such a name builds the spatial topology for its operator also without -s, as in the classic algebra (since 17 October 2026; before, the new algebra stopped with an error).

The expression option must be passed as quoted expression, for example: 

t.select expression="C = A : B"
Where C is the new space time raster dataset that will contain maps from A that are selected by equal temporal relationships to the existing dataset B in this case.

TEMPORAL VECTOR ALGEBRA

The temporal algebra provides a wide range of temporal operators and functions that will be presented in the following section.

TEMPORAL RELATIONS

Several temporal topology relations between registered maps of space time datasets are supported: 
equal             A ------
                  B ------

during            A  ----
                  B ------

contains          A ------
                  B  ----

starts            A ----
                  B ------

started           A ------
                  B ----

finishes          A   ----
                  B ------

finished          A ------
                  B   ----

precedes          A ----
                  B     ----

follows           A     ----
                  B ----

overlapped        A   ------
                  B ------

overlaps          A ------
                  B   ------

over              both overlaps and overlapped
The relations must be read as: A is related to B, like - A is equal to B - A is during B - A contains B

Gebbert, Leppelt and Pebesma (2019) write the relation equal as equals; the new algebra and -c read both names. The previous implementation refused equals with a syntax error.

Topological relations must be specified in {} parentheses.

The relations during and contains are inclusive: during also selects maps that start or finish together with the related map (the relations starts and finishes), and contains also selects maps with the relations started and finished. The relations precedes and follows only apply to maps that touch each other, that is the end of one map is the start of the other. Maps that are separated by a gap have no relation.

TEMPORAL OPERATORS

The temporal algebra defines temporal operators that can be combined with other operators to perform spatio-temporal operations. The temporal operators process the time instances and intervals of two temporal related maps and calculate the result temporal extent by five different possibilities. 
LEFT REFERENCE     l       Use the time stamp of the left space time dataset
INTERSECTION       i       Intersection
DISJOINT UNION     d       Disjoint union
UNION              u       Union
RIGHT REFERENCE    r       Use the time stamp of the right space time dataset

TEMPORAL SELECTION

The temporal selection simply selects parts of a space time dataset without processing raster or vector data. The algebra provides a selection operator : that selects parts of a space time dataset that are temporally equal to parts of a second one by default. The following expression 
C = A : B
means: Select all parts of space time dataset A that are equal to B and store it in space time dataset C. The parts are time stamped maps.

In addition the inverse selection operator !: is defined as the complement of the selection operator, hence the following expression 

C = A !: B
means: select all parts of space time time dataset A that are not equal to B and store it in space time dataset (STDS) C.

To select parts of a STDS by different topological relations to other STDS, the temporal topology selection operator can be used. The operator consists of the temporal selection operator, the topological relations, that must be separated by the logical OR operator | and the temporal extent operator. All three parts are separated by comma and surrounded by curly braces: 

{"temporal selection operator", "topological relations", "temporal operator"}

Examples: 

C = A {:, equal} B
C = A {!:, equal} B
We can now define arbitrary topological relations using the OR operator "|" to connect them: 
C = A {:,equal|during|overlaps} B
Select all parts of A that are equal to B, during B or overlaps B.
In addition we can define the temporal extent of the result STDS by adding the temporal operator. 
C = A {:, during,r} B
Select all parts of A that are during B and use the temporal extents from B for C.

The selection operator is implicitly contained in the temporal topology selection operator, so that the following statements are exactly the same: 

C = A : B
C = A {:} B
C = A {:,equal} B
C = A {:,equal,l} B
Same for the complementary selection: 
C = A !: B
C = A {!:} B
C = A {!:,equal} B
C = A {!:,equal,l} B

CONDITIONAL STATEMENTS

Selection operations can be evaluated within conditional statements.
Note A and B can either be space time datasets or expressions. The temporal relationship between the conditions and the conclusions can be defined at the beginning of the if statement. The relationship between then and else conclusion must be always equal. 
if statement                           decision option                        temporal relations
  if(if, then, else)
  if(conditions, A)                    A if conditions are True;              temporal topological relation between if and then is equal.
  if(conditions, A, B)                 A if conditions are True, B otherwise; temporal topological relation between if, then and else is equal.
  if(topologies, conditions, A)        A if conditions are True;              temporal topological relation between if and then is explicit specified by topologies.
  if(topologies, conditions, A, B)     A if conditions are True, B otherwise; temporal topological relation between if, then and else is explicit specified by topologies.

Comparison operator

The conditions are comparison expressions that are used to evaluate space time datasets. Specific values of temporal variables are compared by logical operators and evaluated for each map of the STDS and the related maps. For complex relations the comparison operator can be used to combine conditions:
The structure is similar to the select operator with the extension of an aggregation operator: 
{"comparison operator", "topological relations", aggregation operator, "temporal operator"}

This aggregation operator (| or &) define the behaviour if a map is related the more than one map, e.g for the topological relations 'contains'. Should all (&) conditions for the related maps be true or is it sufficient to have any (|) condition that is true. The resulting boolean value is then compared to the first condition by the comparison operator (|| or &&). As default the aggregation operator is related to the comparison operator:
Comparison operator -> aggregation operator: 
|| -> | and && -> &
Examples: 
Condition 1 {||, equal, r} Condition 2
Condition 1 {&&, equal|during, l} Condition 2
Condition 1 {&&, equal|contains, |, l} Condition 2
Condition 1 {&&, equal|during, l} Condition 2 && Condition 3
Condition 1 {&&, equal|during, l} Condition 2 {&&,contains, |, r} Condition 3

Hash operator

Additionally the number of maps in intervals can be computed and used in conditional statements with the hash (#) operator. 
A{#, contains}B
The hash operator can only be used in the condition of an if statement.

This expression computes the number of maps from space time dataset B which are during the time intervals of maps from space time dataset A.
A list of integers (scalars) corresponding to the maps of A that contain maps from B will be returned.

The result keeps the time stamps of the maps of A. The temporal operators r, u, i and d have no meaning for the count and are rejected with a syntax error; l is the default. 

C = if({equal}, A {#, contains} B > 2, A {:, contains} B)
This expression selects all maps from A that temporally contains more than 2 maps from B and stores them in space time dataset C. The leading equal statement in the if condition specifies the temporal relation between the if and then part of the if expression. This is very important, so we do not need to specify a global time reference (a space time dataset) for temporal processing.

Furthermore the temporal algebra allows temporal buffering, shifting and snapping with the functions buff_t(), tshift() and tsnap() respectively. 

buff_t(A, size)         Buffer STDS A with granule ("1 month" or 5)
tshift(A, size)         Shift STDS A with granule ("1 month" or 5)
tsnap(A)                Snap time instances and intervals of STDS A
A negative size shrinks the maps; the new algebra stops with an error where a map would keep no time, see "Core and classic algebra".

Single map with temporal extent

The temporal algebra can also handle single maps with time stamps in the tmap function. 
tmap()
For example: 
 C = A {:,during} tmap(event)
This statement select all maps from space time data set A that are during the temporal extent of single map 'event'

Spatial vector operators

The module supports the following boolean vector operations:
 Boolean Name   Operator Meaning         Precedence   Correspondent function
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------
 AND            &        Intersection          1      (v.overlay operator=and)
 OR             |        Union                 1      (v.overlay operator=or)
 DISJOINT OR    +        Disjoint union        1      (v.patch)
 XOR            ^        Symmetric difference  1      (v.overlay operator=xor)
 NOT            ~        Complement            1      (v.overlay operator=not)
And vector functions: 
 buff_p(A, size)    	  Buffer the points of vector map layer A with size
 buff_l(A, size)    	  Buffer the lines of vector map layer A with size
 buff_a(A, size)    	  Buffer the areas of vector map layer A with size

Combinations of temporal, vector and select operators

We combine the temporal topology relations, the temporal operators and the spatial/select operators to create spatio-temporal vector operators: 
{"spatial or select operator" , "list of temporal relations", "temporal operator" }

For multiple topological relations or several related maps the spatio-temporal operators feature implicit aggregation. The algebra evaluates the stated STDS by their temporal topologies and apply the given spatio temporal operators in a aggregated form. If we have two STDS A and B, B has three maps: b1, b2, b3 that are all during the temporal extent of the single map a1 of A, then the following overlay calculations would implicitly aggregate all maps of B into one result map for a1 of A: 

C = A {&, contains} B --> c1 = a1 & b1 & b2 & b3
Keep attention that the aggregation behaviour is not symmetric: 
C = B {&, during} A --> c1 = b1 & a1
                        c2 = b2 & a1
                        c3 = b3 & a1

Examples:

Spatio-temporal intersect all maps from space time dataset A with all maps from space time dataset B which have equal time stamps and are temporary before Jan. 1. 2005 and store them in space time dataset D. 
D = if(start_date(A) < "2005-01-01", A & B)
Buffer all vector points from space time vector dataset A and B with a distance of one and intersect the results with overlapping, containing, during and equal temporal relations to store the result in space time vector dataset D with intersected time stamps. 
D = buff_p(A, 1) {&,overlaps|overlapped|equal|during|contains,i} buff_p(B, 1)
Select all maps from space time dataset B which are during the temporal buffered space time dataset A with a map interval of three days, else select maps from C and store them in space time dataset D. 
D = if({contains}, td(buff_t(A, "1 days")) == 3, B, C)

NOTES

Maps that an expression leaves unchanged, such as the maps of A in C = A : B, are registered in the result dataset as they are, not copied. t.remove with the flags -rf on the result dataset therefore also unregisters them from A, and with -df deletes them. Maps of another mapset cannot be deleted; for them t.remove -df stops with an error and leaves the maps as they are.

The same holds for a time function that leaves the time of a map unchanged: C = tsnap(A) registers the maps of A themselves if they have no gaps, and copies only the maps whose time changes. This holds for both algebras.

Core and classic algebra

Since GRASS 8.6, t.vect.algebra computes its expressions with the functions of the Python package grass.temporal.algebra, as t.rast.algebra does. The previous implementation stays available as the classic algebra, so that published results remain reproducible. The classic algebra is a set of rules of the same functions: it reads the syntax of the previous implementation and applies its rules for relations, order and results. The flag -c selects it for one call, the environment variable GRASS_TEMPORAL_ALGEBRA=classic for a whole session or script; GRASS_TEMPORAL_ALGEBRA=core or an unset or empty variable selects the new algebra, and the flag takes precedence over the variable. In Python, TemporalVectorAlgebraParser takes the keyword classic=True for the same purpose.

The two algebras differ in the following points. The examples use four daily maps in A with one area each, and two maps of two days in B that start with A, each with one area that contains the areas of the two maps of A in its time, and time instances in I.

  1. Right reference. A {:,R,r} B and A {op,R,r} B give one map per related pair, with the time stamp of the right map and, for an overlay, the overlay of this pair. The classic algebra keeps only the last pair of a selection, and an overlay combines the left map with all of its related right maps into one map, with the time stamp of the last of them. The first expression gives four maps, the maps of B with the time stamps of A; the classic algebra gives one map per map of B. If a map b1 contains the maps a1 and a2, the second expression gives b1 & a1 with the time stamp of a1 and b1 & a2 with that of a2, the classic algebra b1 & a1 & a2 with the time stamp of a2: 
    C = B {:,contains,r} A
C = B {&,contains,r} A

    The inverse selection A {!:,R,t} B takes only the temporal operator l, because its maps have no related map to combine with; the classic algebra accepts the other operators.

  2. Empty results. An expression that gives no map creates an empty space time dataset; the classic algebra creates none. No daily map of A contains a map of B of two days, so this expression gives an empty dataset: 
    C = A {:,contains} B
  3. Maps without primitives. A computed map without primitives, such as the intersection of two maps without common area, is not registered. The classic algebra registers it, unless the extents of the two maps do not intersect. If a map e1 has two areas, one left and one right of the area of a1, this expression leaves out a1 & e1, where the classic algebra registers it without areas: 
    C = A & E
  4. Precedence. && binds stronger than ||, and conditions can be grouped with parentheses. The classic algebra evaluates conditions from left to right and does not accept parentheses around conditions. The first expression takes all maps of A, because td(A) == 1 is true for each of them; the classic algebra reads it as the second expression and takes none: 
    C = if(td(A) == 1 || td(A) == 2 && td(A) == 3, A)
C = if((td(A) == 1 || td(A) == 2) && td(A) == 3, A)
  5. Conditions joined with or. If a map of the conclusion of an if has several related condition maps, both algebras join their conditions with and; written as {during,|}, the relation joins them with or, a form that the classic algebra does not read. The first expression takes b2, whose maps of A both start after day 1, the second also b1, which contains a map of A that starts on day 2: 
    C = if({during}, start_day(A) > 1, B)
C = if({during,|}, start_day(A) > 1, B)
  6. td() of a time instance is null instead of an error, so a condition on it is neither true nor false. This expression gives no map; the classic algebra stops with an error: 
    C = if(td(I) > 0, I)

    A temporal variable that a map does not have, such as end_doy() of a time instance, is an error in both algebras.

  7. More syntax. The temporal neighbourhood modifier of t.rast.algebra, such as A[1] and A[-1], applies to vector datasets. Comparisons and temporal functions may stand anywhere in a condition, also with the number on the left, in arithmetic and after a minus sign, and a dataset may have the name of a function. The classic algebra reports a syntax error for each of these expressions; the last one intersects a dataset named td with B: 
    C = A[1]
C = A[-1]
C = if(2 < start_day(A), A)
C = if(start_day(A) + 1 > 3, A)
C = if(-start_day(A) < -2, A)
C = td {&,during} B

    The hash operator may also stand in arithmetic within a condition; the classic algebra reads it only directly before a comparison: 

    C = if(A {#,during} B + 1 > 1, A)
  8. Negative buffer. A negative size in buff_t() shrinks the maps by that size at both ends. The new algebra stops with an error if a map would keep no time, that is if its end would fall on or before its start, and for a time instance, which has no duration to shrink. The classic algebra goes on instead: for a map whose end would fall before its start it prints an error message and keeps the old time stamp, and it makes a time instance of a map whose end equals its start. For maps of two days, the first expression shrinks them to one day and the second is an error: 
    C = buff_t(B, "-12 hours")
C = buff_t(B, "-1 day")
  9. merge() of buffers. The new algebra takes two maps with equal content and time stamps as one map. The classic algebra, as the previous implementation, makes a new map for each buffer of buff_p(), buff_l() and buff_a(), and merge() keeps such a map next to an equal one. This expression gives the buffers of the maps of A once in the new algebra and twice in the classic algebra: 
    C = merge(buff_p(A, 1), buff_p(A, 1))
In Python, both algebras raise SyntaxError for an expression they cannot read. For one they cannot compute, such as if(end_doy(I) > 0, I), the new algebra raises ValueError or TypeError, where the classic algebra raises FatalError. Both raise FatalError for a missing dataset and, without overwrite, for an existing result. The tool stops with the error message in both algebras.

Two corrections apply to both algebras, and to t.rast.algebra, t.rast3d.algebra and t.select as well, so -c gives the rules of the previous algebra, not the results of a particular release. First, if({R}, condition, conclusion) reads "condition R conclusion": if({contains}, start_doy(A) > 0, B) gives the maps of B that lie within a map of A. The code that t.vect.algebra shares with t.select read such relations the other way round before; t.vect.algebra of GRASS 8.4 stops with an error on conditions on temporal variables. Second, with a temporal relation beyond equal, the else part of a condition on temporal variables also takes the maps that no condition map is related to. Figure A3 of Gebbert, Leppelt and Pebesma (2019) needs both: A has maps from day 1 to 3, 3 to 5 and 5 to 7 of January 2001, B has daily maps from day 1 to 10, and this expression gives the first two maps of A and the maps of B from day 5 on, as the figure shows: 

C = if({equal|contains}, start_date(A) <= "2001-01-03", A, B)

Results that rely on the previous reading direction or else rule need the GRASS version they were computed with. The section "Core and classic algebra" in the NOTES of t.rast.algebra describes both corrections.

Corrections to the classic algebra

The classic algebra gives the results of the previous implementation, with the exceptions below. In these cases the previous implementation ended in an error that had no reason or reported the wrong error. -c now gives the result described here. The new algebra never had these errors; only the item on month steps concerned the library and with it both algebras. Results of earlier GRASS versions that used one of these forms need a new run.
  1. tsnap() and tshift() of no maps. If the expression inside has no maps, the result is empty like any other empty result: the classic algebra creates no dataset, and an enclosing expression goes on with no maps. Earlier versions ended in an error. No daily map of A contains a map of B of two days, so both expressions give an empty result: 
    C = tsnap(A {:,contains} B)
C = tshift(A {:,contains} B, "1 day")
  2. Increments that the library refuses. An increment of tshift() or buff_t() that does not fit the temporal type, such as a text that is not a time step or a number without unit on absolute time, is a syntax error and leaves no result dataset. The new algebra refuses the same increments with a ValueError: 
    C = tshift(A, "1 DAY")
C = buff_t(A, 3)
  3. Overlay with an unknown number of areas. An intermediate map that buff_p() leaves empty, because the input has no points, has no known number of areas. It counts as a map without areas: v.overlay is not run, a warning says so, and the result is dropped. Earlier versions ran v.overlay on the map, which failed. A has no points, so this expression gives no map: 
    C = buff_p(A, 1) ^ B
  4. Selection with a time instance on relative time. With the temporal operator i, the time stamp of the result is the time instance. Earlier versions failed with a TypeError for a selection on relative time. If A has maps of relative time that contain the time instances of I, this expression gives maps with the instances as time: 
    C = A {:,contains,i} I
  5. A spatial relation without a temporal one. An operator with spatial relation names only, such as A {&,equivalent,l} B, related no map, because maps are related by their temporal relations, and gave an empty result without a message (corrected 17 October 2026). It stops now with the error "equivalent is a spatial relation and needs a temporal one, as in equal|overlap", as in the new algebra. A selection in braces, which refuses spatial names with a syntax error, gives this error too when the names are spatial only. Write a temporal relation beside the spatial one: 
    C = A {&,equal|equivalent,l} B
  6. Month and year steps from the end of a month. tshift() and buff_t() by months or years ended in an error ("day is out of range for month") when a time stamp moved to a day that the target month lacks, in both algebras. Such a step now ends on the last day of that month (corrected 23 October 2026). With a dataset M whose map lasts from 31 January to 31 March 2001, the first expression gives a map from 28 February to 30 April 2001, the second one a map from 31 December 2000 to 30 April 2001. A year step from 29 February 2004 ends on 28 February 2005: 
    C = tshift(M, "1 months")
C = buff_t(M, "1 months")

    A step back by a multiple of 12 months, for example buff_t(A, "24 months") on a map that starts on 1 December, ended in the error "bad month number 0" and now gives a result (corrected 23 October 2026). Years and months of one step count as one step of 12 * years + months months, so "1 years, 11 months" and "23 months" end on the same day.

Differences from the previous implementation

The differences in message texts, in the debug output of the Python parser, in the order of errors and in the error for an existing result that t.rast.algebra lists apply here likewise, as do the items on lists of absolute and relative time and on the relation equals, the two that change the outcome of an expression.
