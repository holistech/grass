DESCRIPTION

t.vect.algebra applies temporal and spatial operations to space time vector datasets (STVDS) and registers the result in a new STVDS. An expression can select maps by their temporal relations, overlay vector maps that are related in time, buffer the points, lines and areas of maps, and shift, snap and buffer the time stamps of maps. The expressions can be nested and evaluated in conditional statements. See the introduction to temporal data processing for space time datasets and temporal relations. t.rast.algebra and t.rast3d.algebra are the same algebra for raster and 3D raster datasets, and t.select selects maps from datasets of every type.

The tool has two required options and two flags:

  • expression holds the statement result = expression. The statement structure is similar to r.mapcalc. result is the name of the new space time vector dataset, which the tool creates in the current mapset, and the right side of the equality sign is an expression of datasets, operators and functions of the temporal vector algebra described below. The tool needs the whole statement as one quoted value: expression="sel_equal = A : B". A dataset in another mapset is named with its mapset (A@t_vect_algebra); a name without mapset is looked up in the current mapset first, then in the mapsets of the search path, and with -c in the current mapset only. The tool does not overwrite an existing result dataset unless the global flag --overwrite is given.
  • basename is the prefix of the names of the vector maps that the tool creates. A numerical suffix separated by an underscore makes the names unique: sel_during_0, sel_during_1. The number counts the maps that are registered: a map that is left out because it has no primitives (see the NOTES) leaves no gap. The classic algebra numbers the maps it registers without a gap as well. A map that an expression leaves unchanged is registered in the result dataset as it is and gets no new name.
  • -s checks the spatial topology of temporally related maps: the algebra then uses two temporally related maps together only if they are also related in space. The default is a temporal evaluation only. The flag applies wherever the algebra evaluates the topology of maps: in selections, in the hash operator, in the conditions of if statements and in comparison operators. The spatial relations of two maps are equivalent (equal spatial extents), cover and covered, overlap, in and contain, and meet. They can also be named in the curly brackets of an operator, for example {&,equal|overlap}; the maps then need this spatial relation in addition to the temporal one. Such a name builds the spatial topology for its operator also without -s. The classic algebra does not read such names and stops with an error. A spatial relation without a temporal one ({&,equivalent}) is an error. The example "Check the spatial topology" shows -s.
  • -c computes the expression with the rules of the classic algebra, as the releases before GRASS 8.6 did, instead of the rules of the algebra core. The flag takes precedence over the environment variable GRASS_TEMPORAL_ALGEBRA=classic; see "Core and classic algebra" in the NOTES. The example "Use the classic algebra" shows -c.

The result dataset has the temporal type of its input datasets. An expression can combine datasets of one temporal type only; the example "Relative time and datasets of another mapset" shows absolute and relative time and datasets of another mapset. If an expression gives no map, the tool creates an empty result dataset and warns that the result is empty; the exit status is 0. The classic algebra creates no dataset for an empty result.

Examples of every operator and function in this manual are in the EXAMPLES section. Their datasets are created by the example "Create the datasets of the examples", which the others build on.

TEMPORAL VECTOR ALGEBRA

The temporal algebra provides a wide range of temporal operators and functions that are presented in the following sections.

TEMPORAL RELATIONS

Several temporal topology relations between registered maps of space time datasets are supported: 
equal             A ------
                  B ------

during            A  ----
                  B ------

contains          A ------
                  B  ----

starts            A ----
                  B ------

started           A ------
                  B ----

finishes          A   ----
                  B ------

finished          A ------
                  B   ----

precedes          A ----
                  B     ----

follows           A     ----
                  B ----

overlapped        A   ------
                  B ------

overlaps          A ------
                  B   ------

over              both overlaps and overlapped

The relations must be read as: A is related to B, like - A is equal to B - A is during B - A contains B

Gebbert, Leppelt and Pebesma (2019) write the relation equal as equals; the new algebra and -c read both names. The releases before GRASS 8.6 refused equals with a syntax error.

Topological relations must be specified in {} parentheses.

The relations during and contains are inclusive: during also selects maps that start or finish together with the related map (the relations starts and finishes), and contains also selects maps with the relations started and finished. The relations precedes and follows only apply to maps that touch each other, that is the end of one map is the start of the other. Maps that are separated by a gap have no relation. The "Temporal selection operators" example shows each of these rules.

TEMPORAL OPERATORS

The temporal algebra defines temporal operators that can be combined with other operators to perform spatio-temporal operations. The temporal operators process the time instances and intervals of two temporal related maps and calculate the result temporal extent by five different possibilities. 
LEFT REFERENCE     l       Use the time stamp of the left space time dataset
INTERSECTION       i       Intersection
DISJOINT UNION     d       Disjoint union
UNION              u       Union
RIGHT REFERENCE    r       Use the time stamp of the right space time dataset

TEMPORAL SELECTION

The temporal selection simply selects parts of a space time dataset without processing vector data. The algebra provides a selection operator : that selects parts of a space time dataset that are temporally equal to parts of a second one by default. The following expression 
C = A : B

means: Select all parts of space time dataset A that are equal to B and store it in space time dataset C. The parts are time stamped maps.

In addition the inverse selection operator !: is defined as the complement of the selection operator, hence the following expression 

C = A !: B

means: select all parts of space time dataset A that are not equal to B and store it in space time dataset (STDS) C.

To select parts of a STDS by different topological relations to other STDS, the temporal topology selection operator can be used. The operator consists of the temporal selection operator, the topological relations, that must be separated by the logical OR operator | and the temporal extent operator. All three parts are separated by comma and surrounded by curly braces: 

{"temporal selection operator", "topological relations", "temporal operator"}

Examples: 

C = A {:, equal} B
C = A {!:, equal} B

We can now define arbitrary topological relations using the OR operator "|" to connect them: 

C = A {:,equal|during|overlaps} B

Select all parts of A that are equal to B, during B or overlaps B. In addition we can define the temporal extent of the result STDS by adding the temporal operator. 

C = A {:, during,r} B

Select all parts of A that are during B and use the temporal extents from B for C.

The selection operator is implicitly contained in the temporal topology selection operator, so that the following statements are exactly the same: 

C = A : B
C = A {:} B
C = A {:,equal} B
C = A {:,equal,l} B

Same for the complementary selection: 

C = A !: B
C = A {!:} B
C = A {!:,equal} B
C = A {!:,equal,l} B

The first and the "Temporal selection operators" example run these selections.

CONDITIONAL STATEMENTS

Selection operations can be evaluated within conditional statements. Note A and B can either be space time datasets or expressions. The temporal relationship between the conditions and the conclusions can be defined at the beginning of the if statement. The relationship between then and else conclusion must be always equal.

  if(if, then, else)

  if(topologies, conditions, A, B)     A if conditions are True, B otherwise; temporal topological relation between if, then and else is explicit specified by topologies.

Comparison operator

The conditions are comparison expressions that are used to evaluate space time datasets. Specific values of temporal variables are compared by logical operators and evaluated for each map of the STDS and the related maps. For complex relations the comparison operator can be used to combine conditions: The structure is similar to the select operator with the extension of an aggregation operator: 
{"comparison operator", "topological relations", aggregation operator, "temporal operator"}

This aggregation operator (| or &) define the behaviour if a map is related the more than one map, e.g for the topological relations 'contains'. Should all (&) conditions for the related maps be true or is it sufficient to have any (|) condition that is true. The resulting boolean value is then compared to the first condition by the comparison operator (|| or &&). As default the aggregation operator is related to the comparison operator: Comparison operator -> aggregation operator: 

|| -> | and && -> &

Examples: 

Condition 1 {||, equal, r} Condition 2
Condition 1 {&&, equal|during, l} Condition 2
Condition 1 {&&, equal|contains, |, l} Condition 2
Condition 1 {&&, equal|during, l} Condition 2 && Condition 3
Condition 1 {&&, equal|during, l} Condition 2 {&&,contains, |, r} Condition 3

Hash operator

Additionally the number of maps in intervals can be computed and used in conditional statements with the hash (#) operator. 
A{#, contains}B

The hash operator can only be used in the condition of an if statement.

This expression computes the number of maps from space time dataset B which are during the time intervals of maps from space time dataset A. A list of integers (scalars) corresponding to the maps of A that contain maps from B will be returned.

The result keeps the time stamps of the maps of A. The temporal operators r, u, i and d have no meaning for the count and are rejected with an error; l is the default. 

C = if({equal}, A {#, contains} B > 2, A {:, contains} B)

This expression selects all maps from A that temporally contains more than 2 maps from B and stores them in space time dataset C. The leading equal statement in the if condition specifies the temporal relation between the if and then part of the if expression. This is very important, so we do not need to specify a global time reference (a space time dataset) for temporal processing. The "Conditions" example runs this expression, a comparison operator and the rejected temporal operator.

Furthermore the temporal algebra allows temporal buffering, shifting and snapping with the functions buff_t(), tshift() and tsnap() respectively. 

buff_t(A, size)         Buffer STDS A with granule ("1 month" or 5)
tshift(A, size)         Shift STDS A with granule ("1 month" or 5)
tsnap(A)                Snap time instances and intervals of STDS A

A negative size shrinks the maps; the new algebra stops with an error where a map would keep no time, see "Core and classic algebra".

tsnap() ends a last map that is a time instance one granularity of the maps after its start. A granularity of months counts from the first map, with the cut to the end of a shorter month: the time instances January 31, February 28 and March 31 have the granularity of one month, and the last one ends on April 30; after an instance on April 30 in the same series it ends on May 31. The classic algebra (-c) computes the granularity by the rules of the releases before GRASS 8.6 with the correction of item 11 under "Corrections to the classic algebra", one day here, and ends the instance of March 31 on April 1.

The function merge(A, B) gives the maps of both datasets in one dataset.

Single map with temporal extent

The temporal algebra can also handle single maps with time stamps in the tmap function. 
tmap()

For example: 

 C = A {:,during} tmap(event)

This statement select all maps from space time data set A that are during the temporal extent of single map 'event'

Combinations of temporal, vector and select operators

We combine the temporal topology relations, the temporal operators and the spatial/select operators to create spatio-temporal vector operators: 
{"spatial or select operator" , "list of temporal relations", "temporal operator" }

For multiple topological relations or several related maps the spatio-temporal operators feature implicit aggregation. The algebra evaluates the stated STDS by their temporal topologies and apply the given spatio temporal operators in a aggregated form. If we have two STDS A and B, B has three maps: b1, b2, b3 that are all during the temporal extent of the single map a1 of A, then the following overlay calculations would implicitly aggregate all maps of B into one result map for a1 of A: 

C = A {&, contains} B --> c1 = a1 & b1 & b2 & b3

Keep attention that the aggregation behaviour is not symmetric: 

C = B {&, during} A --> c1 = b1 & a1
                        c2 = b2 & a1
                        c3 = b3 & a1

Expressions of the paper

Spatio-temporal intersect all maps from space time dataset A with all maps from space time dataset B which have equal time stamps and are temporary before a given date and store them in space time dataset D. 
D = if(start_date(A) < "2005-01-01", A & B)

Buffer all vector points from space time vector dataset A and B with a distance of one and intersect the results with overlapping, containing, during and equal temporal relations to store the result in space time vector dataset D with intersected time stamps. 

D = buff_p(A, 1) {&,overlaps|overlapped|equal|during|contains,i} buff_p(B, 1)

Select all maps from space time dataset B which are during the temporal buffered space time dataset A with a map interval of three days, else select maps from C and store them in space time dataset D. 

D = if({contains}, td(buff_t(A, "1 days")) == 3, B, C)

The "Conditions" example runs the first and the third form on the datasets of the first example, the first with an empty result, as all maps start after 2005. The example "Expression of the paper with points" runs the second form on datasets of points.

NOTES

Maps that an expression leaves unchanged, such as the maps of A in C = A : B, are registered in the result dataset as they are, not copied. t.remove with the flags -rf on the result dataset therefore also unregisters them from A, and with -df deletes them. Maps of another mapset cannot be deleted; for them t.remove -df stops with an error and leaves the maps as they are.

The same holds for a time function that leaves the time of a map unchanged: C = tsnap(A) registers the maps of A themselves if they have no gaps, and copies only the maps whose time changes. This holds for both algebras.

A computed map without primitives, such as the intersection of two maps without common area, is not registered. When an intermediate map of a longer expression has no areas, the tool leaves out the result map that depends on it and warns with the message "Result map <name> is left out: intermediate map <name> has no areas"; A | (B & A) in the "Overlay operators" example does this for its third map. The computed maps after a left-out map take its number; a map registered unchanged keeps its name.

Core and classic algebra

Since GRASS 8.6, t.vect.algebra computes its expressions with the functions of the Python package grass.temporal.algebra, as t.rast.algebra does. The implementation used before, the reference implementation in grass.temporal.classic, stays in the source code, and its rules stay available as the classic algebra, so that published results remain reproducible. The classic algebra is a set of rules of the same functions: it reads the syntax of the reference implementation and applies its rules for relations, order and results. The flag -c selects it for one call, the environment variable GRASS_TEMPORAL_ALGEBRA=classic for a whole session or script; GRASS_TEMPORAL_ALGEBRA=core or an unset or empty variable selects the new algebra, and the flag takes precedence over the variable. In Python, TemporalVectorAlgebraParser takes the keyword classic=True for the same purpose.

The two algebras differ in the following points. The examples use four daily maps in A with one area each, and two maps of two days in B that start with A, each with one area that contains the areas of the two maps of A in its time, and time instances in I.

  1. Right reference. A {:,R,r} B and A {op,R,r} B give one map per related pair, with the time stamp of the right map and, for an overlay, the overlay of this pair. The classic algebra keeps only the last pair of a selection, and an overlay combines the left map with all of its related right maps into one map, with the time stamp of the last of them. The first expression gives four maps, the maps of B with the time stamps of A; the classic algebra gives one map per map of B. If a map b1 contains the maps a1 and a2, the second expression gives b1 & a1 with the time stamp of a1 and b1 & a2 with that of a2, the classic algebra b1 & a1 & a2 with the time stamp of a2: 
    C = B {:,contains,r} A
C = B {&,contains,r} A

    The inverse selection A {!:,R,t} B takes only the temporal operator l, because its maps have no related map to combine with; the classic algebra accepts the other operators.

  2. Empty results. An expression that gives no map creates an empty space time dataset; the classic algebra creates none. No daily map of A contains a map of B of two days, so this expression gives an empty dataset: 
    C = A {:,contains} B
  3. Maps without primitives. A computed map without primitives, such as the intersection of two maps without common area, is not registered. The classic algebra registers it, unless the extents of the two maps do not intersect. If a map e1 has two areas, one left and one right of the area of a1, this expression leaves out a1 & e1, where the classic algebra registers it without areas: 
    C = A & E
  4. Precedence. && binds stronger than ||, and conditions can be grouped with parentheses. The classic algebra evaluates conditions from left to right and does not accept parentheses around conditions. The first expression takes all maps of A, because td(A) == 1 is true for each of them; the classic algebra reads it as the second expression and takes none: 
    C = if(td(A) == 1 || td(A) == 2 && td(A) == 3, A)
C = if((td(A) == 1 || td(A) == 2) && td(A) == 3, A)
  5. Conditions joined with or. If a map of the conclusion of an if has several related condition maps, both algebras join their conditions with and; written as {during,|}, the relation joins them with or, a form that the classic algebra does not read. The first expression takes b2, whose maps of A both start after day 1, the second also b1, which contains a map of A that starts on day 2: 
    C = if({during}, start_day(A) > 1, B)
C = if({during,|}, start_day(A) > 1, B)
  6. td() of a time instance is null instead of an error, so a condition on it is neither true nor false. This expression gives no map; the classic algebra stops with an error: 
    C = if(td(I) > 0, I)

    A temporal variable that a map does not have, such as end_doy() of a time instance, is an error in both algebras.

  7. More syntax. The temporal neighbourhood modifier of t.rast.algebra, such as A[1] and A[-1], applies to vector datasets. Comparisons and temporal functions may stand anywhere in a condition, also with the number on the left, in arithmetic and after a minus sign, and a dataset may have the name of a function. The classic algebra reports a syntax error for each of these expressions; the last one intersects a dataset named td with B: 
    C = A[1]
C = A[-1]
C = if(2 < start_day(A), A)
C = if(start_day(A) + 1 > 3, A)
C = if(-start_day(A) < -2, A)
C = td {&,during} B

    The hash operator may also stand in arithmetic within a condition; the classic algebra reads it only directly before a comparison: 

    C = if(A {#,during} B + 1 > 1, A)
  8. Negative buffer. A negative size in buff_t() shrinks the maps by that size at both ends. The new algebra stops with an error if a map would keep no time, that is if its end would fall on or before its start, and for a time instance, which has no duration to shrink. The classic algebra goes on instead: for a map whose end would fall before its start it prints an error message and keeps the old time stamp, and it makes a time instance of a map whose end equals its start. For maps of two days, the first expression shrinks them to one day and the second is an error: 
    C = buff_t(B, "-12 hours")
C = buff_t(B, "-1 day")
  9. merge() of buffers. The new algebra takes two maps with equal content and time stamps as one map. The classic algebra, as the reference implementation, makes a new map for each buffer of buff_p(), buff_l() and buff_a(), and merge() keeps such a map next to an equal one. This expression gives the buffers of the maps of A once in the new algebra and twice in the classic algebra: 
    C = merge(buff_p(A, 1), buff_p(A, 1))

In Python, both algebras raise SyntaxError for an expression they cannot read. For one they cannot compute, such as if(end_doy(I) > 0, I), the new algebra raises ValueError or TypeError, where the classic algebra raises FatalError. Both raise FatalError for a missing dataset and, without overwrite, for an existing result. The tool stops with the error message in both algebras.

Two corrections apply to both algebras, and to t.rast.algebra, t.rast3d.algebra and t.select as well, so -c gives the rules of the classic algebra, not the results of a particular release. First, if({R}, condition, conclusion) reads "condition R conclusion": if({contains}, start_doy(A) > 0, B) gives the maps of B that lie within a map of A. The code that t.vect.algebra shares with t.select read such relations the other way round before; t.vect.algebra of GRASS 8.4 stops with an error on conditions on temporal variables. Second, with a temporal relation beyond equal, the else part of a condition on temporal variables also takes the maps that no condition map is related to. Figure A3 of Gebbert, Leppelt and Pebesma (2019) needs both: A has maps from day 1 to 3, 3 to 5 and 5 to 7 of January 2001, B has daily maps from day 1 to 10, and this expression gives the first two maps of A and the maps of B from day 5 on, as the figure shows (see the example "Figure A3 of the publication"): 

C = if({equal|contains}, start_date(A) <= "2001-01-03", A, B)

Results that rely on the previous reading direction or else rule need the GRASS version they were computed with. The section "Core and classic algebra" in the NOTES of t.rast.algebra describes both corrections.

Corrections to the classic algebra

The classic algebra gives the results of the releases before GRASS 8.6, except where those releases were corrected; the page changes in GRASS 8.6 lists all corrections. The items below describe some of them with examples. Item 8 is no correction of those releases: it shows how -c relates maps under -s, which those releases ignored. In the other items those releases computed wrongly, ended in an error that had no reason, or reported the wrong error. -c now gives the result described here. The reference implementation was corrected in the same way, except in items 8 and 9: it still takes the time of a partner whose box only touches, and it still raises the CalledModuleError of a failed module. The new algebra never had these errors; only the items on month steps, on dates beyond the year 9999, on a failed module and on the granularity concerned the library and with it both algebras. Results of earlier GRASS versions that used one of these forms need a new run.
  1. tsnap() and tshift() of no maps. If the expression inside has no maps, the result is empty like any other empty result: the classic algebra creates no dataset, and an enclosing expression goes on with no maps. Earlier versions ended in an error. No daily map of A contains a map of B of two days, so both expressions give an empty result: 
    C = tsnap(A {:,contains} B)
C = tshift(A {:,contains} B, "1 day")
  2. Increments that the library refuses. An increment of tshift() or buff_t() that does not fit the temporal type, such as a text that is not a time step or a number without unit on absolute time, is a syntax error and leaves no result dataset. The new algebra refuses the same increments with a ValueError: 
    C = tshift(A, "1 DAY")
C = buff_t(A, 3)
  3. Overlay with an unknown number of areas. An intermediate map that buff_p() leaves empty, because the input has no points, has no known number of areas. It counts as a map without areas: v.overlay is not run, a warning says so, and the result is dropped. Earlier versions ran v.overlay on the map, which failed. A has no points, so this expression gives no map: 
    C = buff_p(A, 1) ^ B
  4. Selection with a time instance on relative time. With the temporal operator i, the time stamp of the result is the time instance. Earlier versions failed with a TypeError for a selection on relative time. If A has maps of relative time that contain the time instances of I, this expression gives maps with the instances as time: 
    C = A {:,contains,i} I
  5. A spatial relation without a temporal one. An operator with spatial relation names only, such as A {&,equivalent,l} B, related no map, because maps are related by their temporal relations, and gave an empty result without a message. It stops now with the error "equivalent is a spatial relation and needs a temporal one, as in equal|overlap", as in the new algebra. A selection in braces, which refuses spatial names with a syntax error, gives this error too when the names are spatial only. Write a temporal relation beside the spatial one: 
    C = A {&,equal|equivalent,l} B
  6. Month and year steps from the end of a month. tshift() and buff_t() by months or years ended in an error ("day is out of range for month") when a time stamp moved to a day that the target month lacks, in both algebras. Such a step now ends on the last day of that month. With a dataset M whose map lasts from 31 January to 31 March 2001, the first expression gives a map from 28 February to 30 April 2001, the second one a map from 31 December 2000 to 30 April 2001. A year step from 29 February 2004 ends on 28 February 2005: 
    C = tshift(M, "1 months")
C = buff_t(M, "1 months")

    A month step back that ends in December of the year before last or earlier, for example buff_t(A, "24 months") on a map that starts on 1 December or tshift(A, "-13 months") on one that starts in January, ended in the error "bad month number 0" and now gives a result. Years and months of one step count as one step of 12 * years + months months, so "1 years, 11 months" and "23 months" end on the same day.

  7. A date beyond the year 9999. A step that ends behind the year 9999 or before the year 1, for example buff_t(A, "365 days") on a map that ends on 1 December 9999, ended with a traceback ("date value out of range") in both algebras. It now ends with the error "... is beyond the year 9999" or "... is before the year 1"; no map and no dataset is created.
  8. A selection with r or u under -s and maps whose boxes only touch. With -s two maps are related when they are related in time and in space; boxes that meet at an edge or a corner only are not related in space. A selection such as A {:,precedes,r} B takes the time only from maps that are related in time and in space, and an inverse selection such as A {!:,precedes,r} B keeps a map without such a partner with its own time. For example, a map of A from 1 to 2 January whose box meets at a corner the box of a map of B from 2 to 3 January keeps the time 1 to 2 January in the inverse selection above. Without -s only the time relates the maps.
  9. A failed module. If a module fails while the maps are computed, for example v.patch, both algebras ended with a Python traceback. They now end with an error that names the module and its return code, such as Module <v.patch> failed with return code 1, see the error output above; the cause is in the error output of the module above it. No dataset is created.
  10. The relation equals. The releases before GRASS 8.6 refused the spelling equals of the paper with a syntax error. The classic algebra reads it as equal, as the new algebra does.
  11. The granularity of steps over a year and of a time of the day. The granularity of a dataset, which tsnap() uses for the end of a last time instance, counted a step between the first days of two months more than a year apart by the difference of the months only: 1 January 2024 to 1 March 2025 gave 2 months and now gives 14 months. A step whose two ends lie at the same time of the day other than midnight counted in hours or minutes: time instances at 10:30 ten days apart had the granularity 14400 minutes and now have 10 days. A time of the day that differs between the two ends still counts: from 10:30 to 11:30 ten days later the granularity is 241 hours.

Differences from the reference implementation

The differences in message texts, in the debug output of the Python parser, in the order of errors and in the error for an existing result that t.rast.algebra lists apply here likewise, as do the two items that change the outcome of an expression: lists of absolute and relative time, and a relative time instance in a selection.

The examples run in the North Carolina sample dataset nc_spm_08_grass7 from the GRASS sample data. The first example creates the mapset t_vect_algebra and sets the computational region to North Carolina with cells of 1 km; the later examples work in this mapset and region. The algebra works on times and shapes, not on measurements, so the datasets are small sketches of squares, lines and points a few meters wide near the point (300000, 100000) in the region.

Create the datasets of the examples

The examples build on each other and use the same datasets, which this example creates. All vector areas are squares of 10 by 10 meters, made with v.in.region from a small region. The coordinates x and y in the descriptions count meters east and north of the point (300000, 100000), so x = 20 is 300020 in the map; the extent columns of t.vect.list show the full values. The position of a square is given by its west and south edge.
  • A holds the daily maps a1, a2 and a3 from 1 March 2026, squares at x = 0, 20 and 40, y = 0 to 10.
  • B holds the daily maps b1, b2 and b3 of the same days, squares at x = 5, 25 and 70, y = 5 to 15. So b1 overlaps a1 in a square of 5 by 5 meters, area 25, b2 overlaps a2 the same way, and b3 is far from a3.
  • W holds two maps of two days, w1 from 1 to 3 March at x = 5 and w2 from 3 to 5 March at x = 45, y = 0 to 10. In time, w1 contains a1 and a2 (a1 starts together with it, a2 finishes together with it), and w2 contains a3.
  • P holds the daily maps p1, p2 and p3 with one, two and three points at y = 5, ten meters apart, starting at x = 5.
  • L holds the daily maps l1 and l2, each with one line of length 10 meters at y = 5, from x = 0 and x = 20.
  • C holds the maps c1 and c2, copies of w1 and w2 with the same two times. It is the else part of an expression of the paper in the "Conditions" example.
  • The map ev is a square at x = 20 with the time of one day, 2 to 3 March. It is in no dataset; the "Temporal selection" example refers to it with tmap(ev).

Temporal selection operators

Each block selects from A or W and lists the result. The datasets are those of the example "Create the datasets of the examples". The items follow the order of the blocks; item 7 has two blocks.
  1. {:,starts} takes the maps of A that start together with a map of W and end before it: a1 starts w1 and a3 starts w2, so a1 and a3.
  2. {!:,starts} is the complement: a2.
  3. during is inclusive. a1 starts and a2 finishes together with w1, a3 starts together with w2, and {:,during} takes all three.
  4. W {:,contains} A takes the maps of W that contain a map of A, contains including the maps that start or finish together: w1 and w2.
  5. precedes needs maps that touch. tshift(B, "2 days") has the maps from 3 to 4, 4 to 5 and 5 to 6 March. a1 (1 to 2 March) is separated from the first by a gap and gets no partner; a2 ends where the first map starts and a3 where the second starts. The result is a2 and a3.
  6. equals is read as equal, the spelling of the paper.
  7. {:,equal|overlap} combines the temporal relation equal with the spatial relation overlap. a1 and b1 overlap, and so do a2 and b2, but not a3 and b3. The spatial relation is evaluated although the call has no -s: the result has two maps, where A : B has three. The classic algebra does not read spatial names in the braces; the second block, with -c, stops with an error.
  8. Selections are applied from left to right: A : B !: tmap(ev) selects the maps of A that are equal to a map of B, and from them the maps that are not equal to ev, which has the time of a2: a1 and a3.

Overlay operators

The overlay operators combine the maps of two datasets that are related in time, by default equal, with v.overlay or v.patch. The areas of the result maps follow from the squares of the example "Create the datasets of the examples": a1 (x = 0 to 10, y = 0 to 10) and b1 (x = 5 to 15, y = 5 to 15) overlap in a square of 5 by 5. The items follow the order of the blocks; item 6 has two blocks.
  1. A & B is the intersection. The pair a1, b1 has the common area 25, the pair a2, b2 too, and a3 and b3 have none. A map without areas is not registered, so the result has two maps. The second command prints the area of the first result map in square meters.
  2. A | B is the union. The pair a1, b1 gives three areas, 75 only in a1, 25 in both and 75 only in b1; the pair a3, b3 gives its two squares, so the result has the areas 3, 3 and 2.
  3. A ^ B is the symmetric difference: the 75 only in a1 and the 75 only in b1, two areas with a centroid for each overlapping pair. The common square stays in the map as a third area without a centroid, so areas is 3 and centroids is 2.
  4. A ~ B is the complement of B in A: the 75 of a1 outside b1. One area for each pair: a3 does not overlap b3 and stays whole, with the area 100.
  5. P + L patches the maps of two datasets with v.patch: p1 and l1, one point and one line, and p2 and l2, two points and one line. p3 has no partner in L.
  6. A {&,during} W intersects each map of A with the maps of W that contain it. a2 does not touch w1, so its intersection has no areas and is left out, and the result has the maps 0 and 1. The classic algebra, in the second block with -c, numbers them in the same way.
  7. The temporal operator after the relation sets the time of the result maps. r takes the time of the map of W, 1 to 3 and 3 to 5 March; u takes the union of both times, the same here, as the maps of A lie in the maps of W; without an operator the time is that of the left map.
  8. With several related maps the operators aggregate: W {&,contains} A overlays w1 with a1 and a2 into one map, and w2 with a3. The first has no area, as a1 and a2 do not touch, and is left out; the second, ov_agg_0, has the area 50, the square x = 45 to 50.
  9. The overlay operators have the same precedence and are evaluated from left to right, so A | B & A is (A | B) & A: the union of each pair intersected with the map of A, with the areas 2, 2 and 1. With parentheses, A | (B & A) first intersects B and A. a3 & b3 has no areas, so the third map is left out and the result has the areas 2 and 2.
  10. merge(A, B) puts the maps of both datasets into one dataset. The maps of the same day are listed by the order of their names.

Buffers and temporal functions

The buffer functions make areas around the points, lines or areas of each map and keep its time. The size is in meters. The temporal functions change the time of the maps and copy the maps whose time changes.
  1. buff_p(P, 1) buffers the points of p1, p2 and p3: one, two and three circles, as the points are ten meters apart.
  2. buff_p(P, 6) has a radius larger than half the distance of the points, so the circles of one map merge into one area.
  3. buff_l(L, 2) buffers the lines: one area for each map.
  4. buff_a(A, 2) buffers the areas: one area for each map.
  5. The result of a buffer is a vector dataset, so buff_a(buff_p(P, 1), 1) buffers the circles again and keeps their number.
  6. buff_t(W, "1 day") extends each map by one day at both ends: 28 February to 4 March, and 2 to 6 March.
  7. tshift(A, "1 day") moves the maps by one day.
  8. tsnap(A) snaps the maps to a valid topology. A has no gaps, so nothing changes and the result registers the maps a1, a2 and a3 themselves.
  9. tshift(A, "1 day") : A selects the shifted maps that are equal in time to a map of A: the shifted a1 (2 to 3 March) and a2 (3 to 4 March).

Conditions

An if statement takes the maps of its conclusion for which the condition is true. The first argument in braces names the temporal relation between the condition and the conclusion; without it the maps must be equal in time. The items follow the order of the blocks; item 1 has two blocks.
  1. The first form of the paper compares the start date with 1 January 2005. All maps of A start in March 2026, so the condition is false for all of them: the tool warns, and the result dataset D is empty (number_of_maps 0). The second block moves the date to 3 March 2026, so the maps of A before it, a1 and a2, are intersected with the maps of B.
  2. With an else part, the maps of B replace the maps for which the condition is false: b1, as a1 starts on day 1, and a2 and a3.
  3. Conditions join with && and ||: a1 and a2 start in March before the 3rd.
  4. td(A) is the length of each map in days, 1 for all maps of A.
  5. The form of the paper with td() and buff_t(), with the dataset C of the first example as else part: the buffer of 1 day on both sides makes each map of A 3 days long, so the condition is true and the result E holds the maps of B that are within the buffered maps of A, here all three.
  6. With 4 days the condition is false and the else part, C, is the result: c1 and c2.
  7. The relation names the maps of the condition and of the conclusion: {contains} reads "condition contains conclusion". w1 starts on day 1, w2 on day 3, so the condition is true for w2, and the maps of A within w2 are a3.
  8. The hash operator counts the maps of A that W contains: two for w1, one for w2. The condition selects w1, and the conclusion is the same selection with the maps of W that contain a map of A, so the result is w1.
  9. The comparison operator joins two conditions on different datasets: start_day(A) > 1 is true for a2 and a3, start_day(B) < 3 for b1 and b2, and && with the relation equal makes a2 the only match.
  10. The hash operator takes no temporal operator other than l. The block shows the error.
  11. When a map has several related condition maps, the conditions are joined with and. With {during} the maps of A are the condition and the maps of W the conclusion: w1 has a1 (start day 1) and a2 (start day 2), w2 has a3 (start day 3). Joined with and, w1 fails because of a1, so only w2 remains. Written {during,|}, the relation joins the conditions with or, and w1 is true because of a2.

Expression of the paper with points

The expression of the paper that buffers points needs datasets of points: the datasets A and B of the first example hold areas, and buff_p() of a map without points gives nothing, so the result would be empty. The example creates the mapset t_vect_algebra_3 and sets its region. There A and B are new datasets of points, copies of p1, p2 and p3, each with the days 1 and 2 March. A has one and two points and B two and three, ten meters apart. The buffers have the radius 1, so every point has its own circle. The relations in the braces cover every case in which two maps overlap in time, and the temporal operator i gives the result the intersection of both times, here the day. The first day has one common circle, the second two.

        description="Points of the paper expression"

Figure A3 of the publication

Figure A3 of Gebbert, Leppelt and Pebesma (2019) shows the expression of the NOTES (section "Core and classic algebra") on datasets of January 2001. The example creates the mapset t_vect_algebra_4, sets its region and creates new datasets A and B there, so that the expression can be written as it is printed. Their maps are copies of the squares a1 and b1 of the first example: A has three maps of two days from 1 to 7 January 2001, B ten daily maps from 1 to 10 January. The result C holds the first two maps of A, whose start is not after 3 January, and the maps of B from day 5 on, which no such map of A contains or equals. The maps are registered as they are, not copied. The classic algebra (-c) gives the same maps.
