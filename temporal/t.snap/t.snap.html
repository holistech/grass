DESCRIPTION

t.snap closes the gaps between the maps of a space time dataset: it makes the end of every map the start of the next map. Use it to turn time instances (maps with a start and no end) into intervals, or to give maps that overlap or leave gaps a valid temporal topology. Raster, 3D raster and vector datasets are supported, with absolute and relative time. The option type selects the kind of the dataset: strds (the default), str3ds or stvds. The option input names the dataset and is required. See the introduction to temporal data processing for an overview, t.register for giving maps their time and t.shift for moving the maps of a dataset in time.

t.snap takes the maps in the order of their start times. The end of each map becomes the start of the following map, whatever the map had before: a time instance gets an end, a gap before the next map is closed, and an interval that reaches into the next map is cut at its start. The start times do not change.

The last map has no following map. If it is an interval, it keeps its end. If it is a time instance, it ends one granularity of the dataset after its start. The granularity is the one the dataset has before the call, as t.info shows it (the largest step that fits all start and end times of the maps). In a dataset with absolute time, a granularity of months or years is added as calendar months, so maps on the first of January, February, March and April give a last map that ends on May 1. A dataset with a single time instance has no granularity, and the map stays a time instance.

Maps that start at the same time cannot be snapped to each other, as the end of one would be its own start. A map keeps its own end if the map taken after it starts at the same time. Which of two such maps is taken first is not defined. When two maps start together and a map that starts later follows, one of them keeps its end and the other one ends at the start of that later map. Such maps have to be removed or changed to make the topology of the dataset valid.

After the call the dataset is recomputed from its maps: if all maps are intervals now, t.info shows the temporal type of the maps as interval instead of point, and the end time of the dataset is the end of the last map. The time belongs to the map, not to the dataset: a map that is registered in more than one dataset is snapped in all of them, and these datasets are recomputed as well.

A second call changes nothing, as the maps are adjacent after the first one.

NOTES

Relative time works as absolute time, with the unit of the dataset: the last time instance ends one granularity, a number of that unit, after its start.

t.snap writes the new times into the temporal database, so the dataset has to belong to the current mapset. For a dataset of another mapset, also for an empty one, the tool stops with the error Unable to snap dataset <name@mapset> of type strds in the temporal database. The mapset of the database does not match the current mapset, and nothing changes.

A dataset is found by its type and name: a call with a type that does not fit the name stops with Space time dataset <name> of type <stvds> not found, as does an unknown name. Give type=stvds or type=str3ds for the other kinds of datasets.

A dataset without maps is left as it is, and the call ends without an error. Otherwise the call is added to the command history of the dataset (see the flag -h of t.info).

EXAMPLES

Turn time instances into intervals

Four maps are registered on the first day of January to April 2026 with an increment of one month. As t.register is called without -i, they are time instances: their end time is None, and t.info shows the temporal type of the maps as point. The granularity of the dataset is 1 month. After t.snap each map ends on the first of the next month and the last map ends one month after its start, on May 1, which is also the end of the dataset. The maps are intervals now. The granularity is the same as before. A second call of t.snap does not change the list.

Command line:

g.region n=2 s=0 e=3 w=0 res=1
for step in 1 2 3 4; do
    r.mapcalc expression="monthly_$step = $step"
done
t.create output=monthly title="Monthly maps" \
    description="One map on the first day of each month"
t.register input=monthly maps=monthly_1,monthly_2,monthly_3,monthly_4 \
    start=2026-01-01 increment="1 months"
t.rast.list input=monthly columns=name,start_time,end_time
t.info -g input=monthly | grep -E "^(end_time|granularity|map_time)="

Instances at uneven distances

Three maps are time instances on June 1, 3 and 7, 2026. The steps between them are two and four days, so the granularity of the dataset is 2 days. Each map ends where the next one starts, the last one ends two days after its start, on June 9. The intervals have different lengths; t.snap does not make them equal.

Close gaps and cut overlaps between intervals

Four maps are intervals: gap_1 from June 1 to 2, gap_2 from June 4 to 8, gap_3 from June 6 to 7 and gap_4 from June 10 to 12. There is a gap after gap_1 and after gap_3, and gap_2 reaches into gap_3. The method deltagaps of t.rast.list lists the gaps as lines without a name. After t.snap each map ends where the next one starts: gap_1 ends on June 4, gap_2 on June 6, gap_3 on June 10. The last map gap_4 has an end of its own and keeps it. The list has no gaps now, and gap_3 is longer than before.

Command line:

for step in 1 2 3 4; do
    r.mapcalc expression="gap_$step = $step"
done
t.create output=gaps title="Intervals with gaps" \
    description="Four intervals with two gaps and one overlap"
t.register input=gaps maps=gap_1 start=2026-06-01 end=2026-06-02
t.register input=gaps maps=gap_2 start=2026-06-04 end=2026-06-08
t.register input=gaps maps=gap_3 start=2026-06-06 end=2026-06-07
t.register input=gaps maps=gap_4 start=2026-06-10 end=2026-06-12

Snap a dataset with relative time

Three maps are time instances at the hours 0, 3 and 5 of a model run. The steps are three and two hours, so the granularity is 1. The unit of the dataset is hours, and the last map ends at hour 6. The times are numbers of that unit.

for step in 1 2 3; do

t.create output=model temporaltype=relative title="Model run" \

Snap a map that is in more than one dataset

The dataset all holds the time instances shared_1 (June 1), shared_2 (June 2) and shared_3 (June 4); the dataset part holds shared_1 and shared_2 as well. The granularity of all is 1 day. Snapping all gives the maps their ends: June 2, June 4 and June 5. Because the time belongs to the map, part is snapped too, although it was not named: its maps are intervals now, shared_2 ending on June 4 although part has no map after it, and the end time of part follows.

Snap 3D raster and vector datasets

The option type selects the kind of the dataset. Two 3D raster maps are registered on June 1 and June 2, 2026 and two vector maps on the same days. After t.snap with type=str3ds and type=stvds, the first map of each dataset ends on June 2 and the second one day later, on June 3.

Command line:

g.region n=2 s=0 e=2 w=0 t=3 b=0 res=1 res3=1 tbres=1
r3.mapcalc expression="soil_1 = depth()"
r3.mapcalc expression="soil_2 = depth() + 10"
t.create type=str3ds output=soil title="Soil" \
    description="Soil temperature in three layers"
t.register type=raster_3d input=soil maps=soil_1,soil_2 \
    start=2026-06-01 increment="1 day"
for name in gauges_1 gauges_2; do
    v.in.ascii input=- output=$name separator=comma \
        columns="x double precision, y double precision, speed double precision" \
        <<EOF

2.5,1.5,5.0
EOF
done

    description="Wind speed at two gauges"

t.snap type=str3ds input=soil
t.snap type=stvds input=gauges
