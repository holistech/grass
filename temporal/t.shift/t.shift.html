DESCRIPTION

t.shift moves all maps of a space time dataset in time by one amount. Every map is moved by the same amount. Raster, 3D raster and vector datasets are supported; the option type selects the kind of the dataset (strds, str3ds or stvds, the default is strds). Use it to correct a wrong time reference or to move a series onto the time axis of another one. See the introduction to temporal data processing for an overview, t.register for giving maps their time at the start and t.snap for closing the gaps between intervals afterwards.

The option input names the space time dataset, and granularity gives the amount. Both are required. The start and the end of every map are moved by the amount, each for itself; a time instance, which has no end, only moves its start. A map keeps its kind: an interval stays an interval and an instance stays an instance. A negative number moves the maps back in time.

Absolute time

The granularity of an absolute dataset has the form number unit. The number is an integer, and the unit is one of years, months, weeks, days, hours, minutes or seconds; the singular (1 day) is accepted as well. The number alone (5), a fraction (1.5 days) and an unknown unit (1 fortnight) are refused.

A list of such parts separated by commas, such as 1 days, 2 hours, moves the maps by all parts at once. Every part has to be number unit, and every unit may appear once: 1 days, 2 days is refused instead of adding the days. Years and months of a list form one step of 12 * years + months months, with one cut to the last day of the month. The parts from weeks to seconds are added as lengths of time after that.

A step of months or years that ends behind the last day of a month ends on that last day. A map that starts on January 31 and moves by 1 month starts on February 28. The start and the end are moved each for itself, so the length of such a map can change, and a step back does not always lead to the old date. Within a list, the step of months and years comes first: 1 months, 1 days turns January 31 into February 28 and then into March 1.

The time of a dataset as a whole follows its maps: the start and the end of the dataset, and its granularity, are calculated again. A shift by 12 hours of a dataset with daily maps gives it the granularity 24 hours instead of 1 day, because the maps then start at noon and not at midnight.

Relative time

The granularity of a relative dataset is one integer, positive, negative or zero. It is a number of the unit that the dataset has, and the unit cannot be changed by t.shift. A list, a fraction or a text with a unit is refused.

NOTES

The time belongs to the map, not to the dataset. A map that is registered in more than one space time dataset moves in all of them. To shift a part of a dataset only, copy that part into new maps first: t.rast.extract with the option expression makes new maps in a new dataset, and the shift of that dataset does not touch the original maps.

t.shift computes the new time of every map before it changes the first one. A granularity that is refused, a shift that ends behind the year 9999 and a list that names a unit twice therefore leave the dataset as it was. The tool ends with these messages, among others:

  • Wrong granularity format: <granularity> for a granularity that does not fit the temporal type of the dataset or that has a part which is no number unit; a second message names the dataset;
  • Each unit may appear only once in the granularity: <granularity>;
  • Unable to shift dataset <name@mapset> by <granularity>: ... for a shift behind the year 9999, which no date can express. A list names the map whose time cannot be shifted.

A dataset must belong to the current mapset, as t.shift writes the new times into the temporal database. For a dataset of another mapset the tool ends with a message that starts Unable to shift dataset <name@mapset> of type strds and says The mapset of the database does not match the current mapset. The refusal comes before anything is written, also for a dataset without maps.

A dataset without maps is left as it is, also in its command history. t.shift reports that the dataset has no maps, nothing to shift, as a message and ends without an error. Otherwise the call is added to the command history of the dataset (see the flag -h of t.info).

EXAMPLES

Move the maps of a dataset by a length of time

Three maps of daily air temperature form the intervals March 1 to 4, 2026. The shift by 12 hours moves each of them to noon. The dataset then has the granularity 24 hours, while it had 1 day before. A shift by -12 hours brings the maps back, and the granularity with them.

Command line:

g.region n=2 s=0 e=3 w=0 res=1
for step in 1 2 3; do
    r.mapcalc expression="temperature_$step = col() + $step"
done
t.create output=temperature title="Air temperature" \
    description="Daily mean air temperature"
t.register -i input=temperature \
    maps=temperature_1,temperature_2,temperature_3 \
    start=2026-03-01 increment="1 day"
t.rast.list input=temperature columns=name,start_time,end_time
t.info -g input=temperature | grep -E "^(start_time|end_time|granularity)="

Shift by a list of units

Four datasets with one time instance each show how a list is added. plan_a holds the instance February 15, 2003, and plan_b the same day. The list 1 years, 1 months and the single 13 months give the same result, March 15, 2004. The list 1 days, 2 hours then moves plan_a by a day and two hours. plan_c holds January 31, 2001: 1 months, 1 days first moves to February 28 and then adds the day, so the result is March 1. plan_d holds the same day: 1 months, 1 weeks moves to February 28 and then adds seven days, so the result is March 7.

Command line:

for name in a b c d; do

done
t.register input=plan_a maps=point_a start=2003-02-15

t.register input=plan_d maps=point_d start=2001-01-31

t.shift input=plan_b granularity="13 months"
t.shift input=plan_c granularity="1 months, 1 days"
t.shift input=plan_d granularity="1 months, 1 weeks"
for name in a b c d; do

Shift a dataset with relative time

The granularity of a dataset with relative time is an integer in the unit of the dataset. The maps step_1 to step_3 are the intervals 0 to 1, 1 to 2 and 2 to 3 hours. The shift by 10 moves them to the intervals 10 to 11 and so on; the unit stays hours. The shift by -3 leads to 7 to 8 and so on.

Command line:

for step in 1 2 3; do
    r.mapcalc expression="step_$step = $step"
done
t.create output=model temporaltype=relative title="Model" \
    description="Model output per step"
t.register -i input=model maps=step_1,step_2,step_3 start=0 increment=1 \
    unit=hours
t.shift input=model granularity=10
t.rast.list input=model columns=name,start_time,end_time
t.info -g input=model | grep "^unit="

Maps in several datasets, and a part of a dataset

The dataset all_days holds the maps day_1 to day_3, and first_days holds day_1 and day_2 by their names. A shift of first_days by 10 days moves the two maps in both datasets, and all_days shows day_3 unchanged and the other two ten days later. To move a part of a dataset only, t.rast.extract makes new maps: the dataset late_days holds a copy of day_3, which the shift by 100 days moves without touching day_3 in all_days. The extract is made with an expression, which copies the map; the expression day_3 here is the map itself.

Command line:

for step in 1 2 3; do
    r.mapcalc expression="day_$step = $step"
done
t.create output=all_days title="All days" description="Three days"
t.register -i input=all_days maps=day_1,day_2,day_3 start=2026-03-01 \
    increment="1 day"
t.create output=first_days title="First days" description="Two days"
t.register input=first_days maps=day_1,day_2
t.shift input=first_days granularity="10 days"
t.rast.list input=all_days columns=name,start_time,end_time

name|start_time|end_time
day_3|2026-03-03 00:00:00|2026-03-04 00:00:00
day_1|2026-03-11 00:00:00|2026-03-12 00:00:00
day_2|2026-03-12 00:00:00|2026-03-13 00:00:00

t.rast.extract input=all_days output=late_days basename=late \
    where="name = 'day_3'" expression="day_3"
t.shift input=late_days granularity="100 days"
t.rast.list input=late_days columns=name,start_time,end_time

Shift 3D raster and vector datasets

The option type selects the kind of the dataset. The 3D raster maps soil_1 and soil_2 form the dataset soil (type str3ds), and the vector maps gauge_1 and gauge_2 the dataset gauges (type stvds). Both are registered as consecutive days from March 1, 2026 and shifted by 1 month.

Command line:

g.region n=2 s=0 e=2 w=0 t=3 b=0 res=1 res3=1 tbres=1
r3.mapcalc expression="soil_1 = depth()"
r3.mapcalc expression="soil_2 = depth() + 10"
t.create type=str3ds output=soil title="Soil" \
    description="Soil temperature in three layers"
t.register -i type=raster_3d input=soil maps=soil_1,soil_2 \
    start=2026-03-01 increment="1 day"
